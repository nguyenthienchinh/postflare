## When to use
- A customer sent a message, review or email describing a concrete result ("I passed my exam", "we stopped losing plants", "I finally finished level 40 with my kid").
- You want to show who the product is for. A good story makes the right reader think "that's me": stories with identifiable characters and an imaginable plot transport readers, which shifts beliefs and intentions.
- You have a launch or sale coming and want proof in the feed first. People trust recommendations from people they know above every ad channel (89% in Nielsen's 2021 US study); a real customer's words are the closest a brand post gets.

Do not use it without explicit permission, and never paraphrase a quote into something the person did not say. In the US the FTC Endorsement Guides apply: the quote must reflect the customer's honest view and real experience.

## Structure
1. **Permission first.** Get a written yes for: their name or handle, the quote, any photo/screenshot, and the platforms it will appear on, and tell them it is for marketing. Offer anonymity ("a nurse in Leeds"). Ask whether anything connects them to you (free plan, discount, friend, employee); if so, disclose it in the post.
2. **Line 1: the person and the situation, ≤ 90 characters.** "Mara works 12-hour night shifts and kept forgetting her plants." Specific beats impressive.
3. **The problem in their words.** One or two lines. Include what they tried before; it makes the switch believable.
4. **The turning point.** The one feature or moment that helped. One, not five.
5. **The result.** A concrete outcome with a time frame. If there is a number, use theirs, not an estimate. If the result is exceptional, say what users generally get (FTC rule for testimonials).
6. **The quote.** A short verbatim quote (one to three sentences) in quotation marks, credited as agreed.
7. **Call to action.** One soft, clear next step: "Pocket Plants is free on iOS and Android" plus the link where links work, or in a follow-up to keep the story clean.

## Platform notes
- **Instagram:** carousel (up to 10 items via the publishing API; the app allows 20) works well: slide 1 the quote on a plain background with their first name, slides 2–4 situation → feature → result, last slide the app. Caption truncates after roughly the first line or two (~125 characters is a common estimate); no clickable links in captions, so "link in bio". Max 5 hashtags. Tag the customer only if they agreed. Alt text on every slide.
- **TikTok / Reels / Shorts:** if the customer will film themselves, that usually beats anything you can make (unverified: test it). Otherwise, a screen recording of the feature with their quote as on-screen text. 9:16, first 2 seconds = the situation. Links in Shorts descriptions and comments are not clickable (since Aug 2023); point to the channel link or say "search {app name} on the App Store".
- **X:** 280 characters (URLs count 23), so story + quote in the main post. Link in the post or a reply: X says links are no longer deboosted (company claim, unverified: test it), and via the API a post or reply with a URL costs $0.20 either way.
- **Bluesky:** 300 graphemes; a link card in a reply or in the post is fine here.
- **Threads:** 500 characters, one topic tag, up to 5 links. Links are allowed but a reply keeps the story clean.
- **LinkedIn:** 3,000 characters; strongest for B2B/SaaS stories. First two lines must work before "…see more" (roughly 140–210 characters, varies by device). Link in the first comment is common practice (unverified: test it). Mention the customer's company only with permission.
- **Facebook:** longer story is fine; link in the first comment.

## Hooks
- **Who + problem:** "{Name} {specific situation} and {specific problem}." — the reader recognises themselves.
- **Quote first:** "'{short striking quote}' — {name}, {role or context}" — a real voice is trusted more than brand copy; whether it lifts clicks for you is unverified: test it.
- **Tried everything:** "{Name} tried {alternative 1} and {alternative 2} before {app}." — makes the switch credible.
- **Result + time:** "{Result} in {time frame}. Here's what {name} changed." — concrete payoff up front.

The thing that changed it: Shipnote drafts the notes from merged pull requests, and she edits instead of writing.

Six weeks in, release notes take her about 15 minutes per client, and two clients now reply to them with feature ideas.

"I stopped dreading Fridays. That's the whole review." — Priya Nair, founder of Lantern Dev

Link to the setup she uses is in the first comment.

## Avoid
- Posting without written permission, or tagging/naming beyond what was agreed.
- Edited, merged or invented quotes; fake personas; stock photos posed as the customer.
- Vague praise ("Love this app!!!") as the whole story. If it has no situation and no result, it is not a story.
- Implying the result is typical when it is an outlier; the FTC requires saying what users generally get.
- Paying or rewarding a customer only for a positive story, or hiding a connection (free plan, friend, employee). Fake or sentiment-conditioned testimonials are banned under the FTC's 2024 rule.
- Listing every feature; one turning point per story.
- "Link in bio" on platforms where links work.

## Checklist
- [ ] Written permission covers name, quote, images and platforms.
- [ ] Line 1 names the person (or anonymised description) and a specific situation.
- [ ] The quote is verbatim and credited as agreed.
- [ ] One feature or turning point, one concrete result with a time frame.
- [ ] No claim of typical results from one case; exceptional results state what users generally get.
- [ ] Any connection (free plan, discount, friend, employee) is disclosed in the post.
- [ ] One clear call to action; link placed per platform (post or reply on X, first comment on LinkedIn/Facebook, bio on Instagram, channel link on Shorts).
- [ ] Text fits each platform limit and the fold.
- [ ] Alt text is set on images.
