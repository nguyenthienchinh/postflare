## When to use
Use when you know a recurring, specific pain your audience feels and your product (or a technique) fixes it. Best sourced from real language: app reviews, support emails, Reddit threads, replies. The goal is engagement: people who recognise themselves reply "this is me" or share their own version. It works for product posts and for pure-value posts where the solution is a technique, not the app.

## Structure
1. **Line 1, the problem (≤ 70 characters, no emoji first):** the pain as the audience would say it, with a concrete moment. "You open your laptop to work and 40 minutes later you've answered Slack and nothing else."
2. **Lines 2–3, agitate briefly:** one or two lines on the cost or the failed fix people already tried. Keep it short; agitation longer than the solution reads as fear-mongering.
3. **The turn (1 line):** "What fixed it for me:" or "The fix is boring:".
4. **The solution (2–4 lines):** the specific mechanism, not just the product name. If it is the product, show the feature doing the job (screenshot or 5-second clip).
5. **Proof (1 line):** a result, a user quote or your own number, real and attributable.
6. **Close with a real question (optional):** "What's the fix that worked for you?" An open question is fine; Meta demotes posts that explicitly ask for reactions, comments, shares or tags as engagement bait.
7. Any link goes where the platform makes it clickable without costing reach (see notes), usually a `thread` follow-up (first reply / first comment).

## Platform notes
- **X:** 280 chars (Premium 25,000); any URL counts as 23. Problem + turn + solution must fit; proof can go in a reply. Buffer's 2025 data shows near-zero median engagement on link posts from non-Premium accounts (X's head of product disputes a penalty), so put the link in the first reply. The X API bills any post with a URL at $0.20 vs $0.015, reply included.
- **Bluesky:** 300 graphemes; link cards are fine but a reply link keeps the post about the problem.
- **Threads:** 500 chars, one topic tag per post. Conversational tone; the closing question works well here (unverified: test it). Links are allowed in the post.
- **Mastodon:** 500 chars default; links count as 23. CamelCase hashtags (#Productivity) so screen readers read the words. Lead with the technique; product mention second.
- **LinkedIn:** 3,000 chars; the preview cuts after the first 2–3 lines (often cited as ~210 chars, unverified: check on mobile), so the problem line plus the agitation must sit above "…see more". Short lines, white space. On company pages put the link in the first comment; personal profiles show little link penalty in third-party data.
- **Instagram:** caption fold around 125 chars (approximate), so the problem is the first line. Carousel (up to 10 slides via the publishing API): slide 1 problem, slide 2 cost, slides 3–5 solution, last slide proof. Max 5 hashtags, alt text, "link in bio" (caption links aren't clickable).
- **TikTok / Reels / Shorts:** the first 1–3 seconds show the problem happening (on-screen text in the audience's words), then cut to the fix; TikTok advises the value proposition within 3 seconds and the hook within 6. Vertical 9:16, 3–5 hashtags (Reels max 5), Shorts title ≤ 100 chars. If the fix is your app, turn on TikTok's commercial content disclosure ("Your brand"). Shorts description/comment links aren't clickable.
- **Facebook:** longer text is fine; links in the first comment. Meta has tested capping link posts for Pages and professional-mode profiles (Dec 2025); links in comments aren't capped.

## Hooks
- **Their words:** "{exact phrase from reviews or support emails}." — recognition beats cleverness; people stop for their own sentence.
- **The moment:** "It's {time}, you {action}, and {bad thing} happens again." — a concrete scene makes it felt, not described.
- **The failed fix:** "You tried {common fix}. It worked for {short time}. Here's why." — speaks to people already invested in the problem.
- **Cost:** "{Problem} costs you {concrete cost}, and you probably don't notice." — only if the cost is real and explainable.

## Examples
### X (main post + reply)
```
You water your plants every Sunday and they still die.

It's not you. A weekly schedule ignores light: a fern by a north window drinks half what it does by a south one.

The fix: water by light and season, not by calendar. Pocket Plants does that math for you.
```
(260 chars)

Reply:
```
Free for 5 plants: pocketplants.app
```

### LinkedIn
```
"I'll just check one thing" is how you lose a whole morning.

You open the laptop with a plan. Forty minutes later you've cleared Slack and the plan is untouched.

Blocking sites didn't fix it for me. I just found other tabs.

What worked: deciding the one task before opening the laptop, then a 25-minute timer that hides everything else until it ends.

That's the whole idea behind Tempo's focus mode. Since I started, my first deep-work block happens before 10am four days out of five.

What's the habit that finally fixed your mornings?
```

### TikTok (on-screen text + caption)
```
On-screen 0-2s: "When you place a tower and instantly regret it"
On-screen 3-8s: undo button, tower refunded
Caption: Misplaced towers were the #1 complaint in our reviews, so 2.0 has undo. #towerdefense #indiegame #mobilegames
Disclosure: Commercial content on, "Your brand"
```

## Avoid
- Vague problems ("struggling with productivity?"); name the moment.
- Agitation longer than the solution, or fear-based exaggeration.
- Inventing a problem the product happens to solve; use real audience language.
- Hiding the solution behind "link in bio" or "DM me" when it can be explained in the post.
- Engagement bait like "comment YES if this is you" or "like if you agree"; Meta demotes it.
- Links in the X main post, or in the body of a LinkedIn company page post.
- Pasting the same caption to every platform.
- Fake testimonials or unattributable proof.

## Checklist
- [ ] The first line names one specific problem in the audience's words, ≤ 70 characters.
- [ ] Agitation is two lines or fewer.
- [ ] The solution explains the mechanism, not only the product name.
- [ ] Proof is real and attributable.
- [ ] Any question at the end is genuine, not "comment YES".
- [ ] The link sits in a `thread` follow-up on X, LinkedIn pages and Facebook; in bio on Instagram/TikTok.
- [ ] Each platform version fits its limit, fold and hashtag cap.
- [ ] TikTok commercial content disclosure is on when the fix is your app.
- [ ] Visuals have alt text; video shows the problem in the first 2 seconds.
